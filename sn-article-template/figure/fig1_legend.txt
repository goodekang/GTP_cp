Figure 1 | Bio-STFM: A multimodal framework for bridging spatial transcriptomics and histopathology with biological constraints.

a, Overall architecture of Bio-STFM. Whole slide images (WSIs) are tessellated into 256×256 pixel patches and encoded via a pretrained vision encoder (φ_H; ViT-H/14 with CONCH weights, see Supplementary Fig. S2a for backbone comparison) to obtain patch-level embeddings H ∈ ℝ^(N×D). Paired single-cell or bulk RNA-seq data is processed through Geneformer (φ_G) to generate genomic embeddings G ∈ ℝ^(M×D). These modalities are integrated via a knowledge-guided co-attention mechanism, where the attention matrix A = Softmax(QK^T) is constrained by a biological mask derived from prior protein–protein interaction (PPI) networks. The resulting representations are processed by a hierarchical graph transformer comprising parallel WSI (𝒯_H) and Genomic (𝒯_G) transformer branches. Features are combined via average pooling and a multilayer perceptron (MLP) risk head to predict patient-level risk scores.

b, The data paradox and domain gap in computational pathology. High-quality spatially-resolved molecular profiling (e.g., HTAN) provides rich biological depth but limited sample sizes (N), whereas large-scale clinical cohorts (e.g., TCGA H&E slides) offer statistical power but lack molecular resolution. Bio-STFM employs domain adaptation as a bridge to transfer knowledge across this domain gap, enabling inference on the unobserved paired region.

c, Multi-scale graph construction. The model represents tissue microenvironment across three hierarchical levels: L0 (Gene Network) captures gene regulatory relationships; L1 (Cell Communities) models cellular neighborhoods and local microenvironment structure; L2 (Tissue Motif) represents higher-order spatial patterns. Nodes represent cells or genes, while edges encode physical proximity or regulatory interactions.

d, In silico perturbation analysis. The framework computes ΔRisk = f(G) − f(G'), where G represents the original graph and G' represents the perturbed graph after blocking specific cell-cell or ligand–receptor interactions (indicated by red X). Positive ΔRisk indicates the blocked interaction increases predicted risk (potential therapeutic hypothesis); negative ΔRisk suggests a protective interaction. This approach prioritizes candidate therapeutic targets by quantifying prediction sensitivity to specific interactions.

Abbreviations: WSI, whole slide image; PPI, protein-protein interaction; MLP, multilayer perceptron; HTAN, Human Tumor Atlas Network; TCGA, The Cancer Genome Atlas.
