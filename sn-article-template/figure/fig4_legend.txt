Figure 4 | Mechanistic interpretation of spatial features driving survival prediction.

a, Whole slide image (WSI) attention map. Representative breast cancer slide with overlaid attention weights learned by Bio-STFM. Warmer colors (orange/red) indicate regions receiving higher attention during risk prediction (predicted risk score = 0.725 for this case). Two regions of interest (ROI-1, ROI-2) are highlighted with dashed boxes for detailed analysis. Scale bar, 1 mm. The model allocates attention to prognostically informative regions including tumor-stroma interface and areas with specific immune cell configurations; high attention indicates relevance for prediction, not necessarily high risk per se (see Supplementary Fig. S4c for population-level attention-risk relationship).

b, Perturbation stability analysis. Heatmap showing rank correlation between original and perturbed attention rankings across different perturbation types (Noise, Dropout, Shuffle, Edge removal, Node removal) and levels (1%–50%). High correlations (>0.89) across most conditions demonstrate that the model's interpretations are robust to input perturbations and not artifacts of overfitting. Values indicate Spearman rank correlation coefficients.

c, Cell community structure in a representative high-risk region. Spatial scatter plot showing the distribution of n = 400 cells colored by cell type: Tumor (pink), cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAF, brown), CD8+ T cells (teal), CD4+ T cells (yellow), Macrophages (orange), B cells (blue), Endothelial cells (green), and NK cells (magenta). The dashed red circle highlights a high-risk region characterized by immune exclusion patterns. Coordinates in micrometers (μm).

d, Attention entropy comparison across model variants. Violin plots showing the distribution of attention entropy for the full model (Ours), and ablated versions removing biological constraints (−Mask), spatial structure (−Spatial), or using random attention (Random). Lower entropy indicates more focused, interpretable attention patterns. The full model achieves significantly lower entropy than all ablated variants (***P < 0.001, two-sided Wilcoxon test).

e, Attention entropy across transformer layers. Line plot tracking entropy evolution through layers 1, 3, and 6 of the transformer. The full model (Ours, teal diamonds) maintains consistently lower entropy compared to −Mask (orange), −Spatial (teal circles), and Random (gray) variants, indicating that biological and spatial constraints promote focused attention throughout the network hierarchy.

f, Immune exclusion barrier motif. Discovered spatial motif associated with poor prognosis. The network diagram illustrates CAF cells forming a physical barrier around CD8+ T cells, preventing their infiltration into the tumor core. TGFβ-TGFBR signaling (pink arrow) mediates this barrier effect. Blocking this interaction is predicted to restore immune access. HR = 2.15, P = 0.001, indicating patients with this motif have significantly worse survival.

g, Motif-survival association. Box plots comparing the frequency of immune exclusion motifs between short survival (n = 81) and long survival (n = 119) patient groups. Patients with short survival harbor significantly more immune exclusion motifs (P < 0.0001, ***two-sided Mann-Whitney U test), validating the prognostic relevance of the discovered spatial pattern.

h, Gene-morphology correlation analysis. Heatmap showing Pearson correlation coefficients (r) between marker gene expression and histopathological features. Notable associations include: Pleomorphism correlates with ERBB2 (r = 0.62) and TP53 (r = 0.73); Nuclear Area correlates with TP53 (r = 0.24); Stromal Ratio correlates with VIM (r = 0.31). Asterisks indicate statistical significance (*P < 0.05, Bonferroni-corrected). These associations link molecular alterations to morphological phenotypes observable in routine H&E staining.

Abbreviations: WSI, whole slide image; ROI, region of interest; CAF, cancer-associated fibroblast; NK, natural killer; TGFβ, transforming growth factor beta; TGFBR, TGFβ receptor; HR, hazard ratio; H&E, hematoxylin and eosin.
